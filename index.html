China’s Kicking Away The Ladder Principle v/s India’s Vishwaguru Mantra - Report by Yuvraj Singh

Introduction: In the current geopolitical order of the world, it is crystal clear that China (Dual Circulation Strategy) and India (PLI) don’t go hand-in-hand with each other in terms of Trade, although they share the same motive of encouraging the domestic manufacturing (India) and its circulation (China). In China’s “White Fox” visit to India in August 2025, it was imminent that he was there to take the better advantage of the tussle of Trump’s Tariff on import. China offered a large amount of Trade, offering Rare Earth Minerals along with DAP, a highly demanded fertiliser with immense amount of nutrients for agricultural use. Later, the deal was not finalised during Wang Yi’s visit to India but China agreed in-principle to address India’s long standing concerns regarding these supplies. China’s Complaint to WTO on India’s PLI Scheme: —To analyse the cause-and-effect relationship of this complaint, we need to understand why was PLI scheme launched? After Hon. Prime Minister Shri Narendra Modi took over the administration after Congress’s Manmohan Singh, within the first two quarters of his tenure, the Make In India initiative was launched to encourage the manufacturing of goods domestically, the share of all Make In India goods was just 15.06% to 16.7%, the Make In India scheme was instigated to inflate the share from 15-16% to 25%. Now you wonder how can this initiative grow so drastically?(~9% growth), this was to be achieved by creating SEZs, locations offering substantial subsidies to companies. For Example: Samsung’s largest manufacturing plant is located in Noida, UP. The opening of such plants helps India to “add value” ie. Help in VALUE ADDITION. After this launch, the Chinese companies viewed it as a green signal to enter into the Indian Economy, tech giants like Xiaomi entered India in 2014. In the late 2016-17, the Make In India initiative started to look like a failed attempt! To support this Idea, several other schemes were launched, one of which was PLI (Production-Linked Incentive), launched in Early 2020. -PLI was launched to transform India’s economic structure from a service-relied economy to a industrial giant, this was itself launched under the Atmanirbhar Bharat Scheme, India relied heavily on foreign nations primarily China, for foundational industrial inputs. For instance, despite being a global hub for generic medicines, India imported nearly 70% of its API bulk drugs from China. The other motive of launching PLI was to compensate for the handicaps of manufacturing, such as Infra-related, Tariffs, Transport. For Example: Let’s Assume, The cost of production of a good in China is $100, the cost of the same in India would be $110, the 10% increase accounts for the difference in the level of technology, transport (China has started to EV-ify its transport, for optimised cost cutting. From a economical view, India needed either a large share of Manufacturing or Assembling Industry in its GDP to solve some serious problems, 1st was Unemployment, 2nd was Non-Domestic Production, 3rd was a Stagnant GDP. Amazingly, all of these problems could be solved with the launch of PLI, which would reward the industrialists with 4-6% of cash incentive on sales made on domestically produced goods. How does the launch of PLI affect China? India, through PLI, targeted the core 14 sectors of manufacturing, including Pharmaceuticals, IT, Electronics etc. The effect of the launch was so drastic that it affected the Chinese production units to its core, to understand this, let’s see the example of Apple Inc: Before the Launch of PLI, the largest manufacturing plant of Apple Inc. was located in Chinese cities like Shenzhen and Zhangzou. But afterwards, due to SEZs and PLI, Apple's largest strategic plan in India is transforming the country into its second global manufacturing and export headquarters, bypassing geopolitical tariffs. Thus, they opened Their factories in South India; Tamil Nadu, Karnataka etc. Changes like these happened in many fields and forced a value addition in Indian economy, and as value addition increased, the export of goods also increased, for a comparison: So, after the effect of PLI, The goods produced by India was produced at the cost of the goods produced by China. India introduced multiple Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes specifically designed to boost the electric vehicle (EV) sector, allocating a budgetary outlay of a behemoth 25,938 Crore Rupees for the same, providing 8-18% Incentive on a variety of components of an electric vehicle, Some other technological updates are listed below: -Introduction of the VIKRAM CHIP: This was a state-of-the-art technological advancement in the field of deep space research as this was the first ever homogenous chip used to manueavering and directive calculations.aa -ACC Battery Cell Storage: Launched with the budgetary outlay of 18,100 Crore Rupees, aims to establish a competitive domestic manufacturing ecosystem for 50 GWh (Gigawatt hour) of ACC batteries, used for many technologies that use electric batteries, it also aimed to reduce reliance on imported batteries, lower overall EV production costs, and secure a reliable supply chain for domestic automakers. All these technological advancements started to challenge the monopoly of Chinese EV Industry, manufacturers like BYD, originating from China, were one of the most advance companies, and the advancements in India were challenging the threshold of their Monopoly in the tech, This was the main reason of China running to the WTO to file a complaint against India for FTP! “China—India trade dispute: Beijing seeks WTO panel against India’s auto, battery and EV incentives afters talks fail” The above mentioned text is the headline of Times of India on the China India Dispute on PLI. The Purpose of WTO: World Trade Organisation (WTO), was founded in 1995 replacing GATT (General Agreement of Trade and Tariffs), its purpose is to monitor fair trade globally, it is a universal body for trade across the world and any hindrances in trade shall be reported to WTO for resolvement, it has a certain set of practices which it terms as FAIR TRADE PRACTICES (FTPs). In situation like that of China and India: China has reported to WTO that India is using SCM (Subsidies and Countervailing Measures) and its use is damaging the economy of China, calling it a Protectionist Policy of Trade The fact of Irony is that, China itself is empowering its own domestic EV Industry, by launching numerous policies, China had already violated the FTPs when it stopped the supply chain of REEs, also it has ended all the Subsidies it used to give on EVs in its own country, this could also harm the Indian Economy if they hadn’t developed ACC and Vikram Chip. China has launched its own policy in 2025, IRONICALLY CALLING IT “MAKE IN CHINA” especially for infant industries, which in turn makes it a PROTECTIONIST POLICY, which works against the rules of WTO, using the Kicking Away The Ladder Principle ie, not allowing India to enter into the market and end its monopoly. Conclusion: To conclude everything that has been discusses above, I would like to add that the Chinese Government’s action are a classic example of Kicking Away the Ladder Principle. Earlier, it had tried to monopolise the EV industry, which it did for nearly a decade but now, when India tried to challenge its majority in the market, it ran to the WTO to complain against India’s PLI Scheme.
